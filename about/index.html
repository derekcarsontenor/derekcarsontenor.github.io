Derek Carson is a buffo tenor who hails from the suburban haven of Boise, Idaho. He has had a deep love for performing and being the center of attention ever since he was a child. He is known for enthralling audiences with his captivating interpretations of comedic characters, larger-than-life physical comedy, and bright vocals.

Derek is currently pursuing a Master’s degree in Opera Performance at Arizona State University, studying under Carole FitzPatrick. He is an ASU Vocal Teaching Assistant, teaching both group singing classes and private voice lessons. In 2025, he completed a Bachelor’s degree in Vocal Performance at the Lionel Hampton School of Music at the University of Idaho, where he studied with Dr. Michelle Lange.

Derek is proud to have worked as a resident artist with Opera Idaho for their 2024–2025 season, performing as Giuseppe in Verdi’s La Traviata and as Pinellino in Puccini’s Gianni Schicchi. He has also appeared as a soloist with Gemma Arts—a classical production and booking company based in the Treasure Valley—in both their choral and early music ensembles.

Collegiate performance credits include Bill in Jonathan Dove’s Flight, Christopher Belling in Kander and Ebb’s Curtains, La théière in Ravel’s L’Enfant et les sortilèges, Alfredo in Johann Strauss’ Die Fledermaus, Frederick in Gilbert and Sullivan’s The Pirates of Penzance, and Tamino in Mozart’s Die Zauberflöte.

Derek’s choral and oratorio credits include appearances as a soloist in Handel’s Messiah, Craig Hella Johnson’s Considering Matthew Shepard, and Bach’s Ein feste Burg ist unser Gott.

As a lover of many vocal genres, Derek has performed in multiple jazz choirs, singing lead on standards such as “Mister Five-by-Five,” “One Less Bell to Answer,” and “Everyday I Have the Blues.”

Outside of music, Derek has a few unexpected talents, including the inexplicable ability to do the splits, an affinity for backbends, a love of dance and choreography, and an extensive knowledge of back-cracking techniques.
